Why Is a Millage Adjustment Needed?

The Riverview Fire Department serves residents and businesses within our special tax district in Fort Mill. Here are the core factors driving the referendum:

Population & Call Growth

Fort Mill has seen rapid residential and commercial expansion over the past decade. Emergency call volume—including structure fires, medical first responder calls, and vehicle accidents—has increased substantially.

  • Higher volume of medical emergency calls
  • Increased traffic and accident responses

Equipment & Supply Costs

The cost of essential fire trucks, protective turnout gear, breathing apparatus (SCBA), and advanced medical equipment has surged over 40% due to national supply chain inflation.

  • Fire engine costs up significantly
  • Gear must meet mandatory safety standards

24/7 Professional Coverage

Ensuring round-the-clock shift staffing requires competitive compensation, healthcare benefits, and ongoing specialized training to retain highly trained firefighters in our area.

  • Fast, reliable response times
  • Maintains ISO rating for insurance savings

Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Here are answers to the most common questions regarding the Riverview Fire Department millage referendum.

The referendum asks voters within the Riverview Special Tax District in Fort Mill, SC, to approve an increase to the maximum millage cap for fire protection services. Currently, our tax district millage is legally capped at 10 mills. The referendum allows the district to adjust the millage up to the proposed new cap to fund essential fire department operations, staffing, and equipment.

A mill represents $1 of tax for every $1,000 of a property’s assessed value (not market value). In South Carolina, an owner-occupied primary residence is assessed at 4% of market value. For example, a $300,000 home has an assessed value of $12,000 ($300,000 × 0.04). Therefore, 1 mill equals exactly $12 per year (or $1.00 per month) for a $300,000 primary home.

The 10-mill cap was established years ago when our population was a fraction of what it is today. Rapid commercial and residential growth in Fort Mill has dramatically increased emergency calls. At the same time, inflation has raised the cost of fire engines, turnout gear, safety equipment, and competitive pay required to maintain 24/7 professional staffing.

Insurance companies determine homeowner insurance premiums largely based on a fire department’s ISO (Insurance Services Office) Public Protection Classification rating. Well-equipped departments with adequate staffing, fast response times, and modern equipment earn lower ISO scores. Maintaining a top ISO rating directly reduces annual home insurance premiums for residents across our district, often saving homeowners more money on insurance than the millage increase costs.

No. Voting "YES" authorizes the Fire District Board to set the annual millage up to the approved cap as necessary during the annual budgeting process. Millage is evaluated each fiscal year transparently to ensure only the revenue needed for operating expenses, staffing, and capital improvements is levied.

Any registered voter residing within the boundaries of the Riverview Special Tax District in Fort Mill / York County, SC is eligible to vote on this ballot measure.

All tax revenue collected in the Riverview Tax District is strictly dedicated to fire protection and emergency services. Funds are subject to public oversight, open budget hearings, and independent annual financial audits through York County fiscal management.

Under South Carolina law, referendum petitions require registered voters residing in the Riverview Fire District to provide an original, in-person ink signature.
